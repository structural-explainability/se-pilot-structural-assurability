## Source Structure

The framework describes a method for constructing
customized assessments of AI systems.

Its assessment structure includes:

- Blocks representing measurement concepts of interest.
- Events and Tools used to test AI systems.
- Data produced by those assessment activities.

These are the source's concepts, not definitions
introduced by Structural Assurability.

## Evaluative Claim

Researcher-formulated question:

Does the evidence produced by a specified TEVV-Athlon
assessment support the distinctions necessary to
evaluate its intended claim?

No particular NIST assessment or claim has yet been
selected for detailed evaluation.

## Required Distinctions

The investigation will identify two or more
claim-relevant alternatives.

It will then determine whether the selected assessment
would produce distinguishable observations for those
alternatives under specified conditions.

The alternatives must be derived from a concrete
assessment question rather than assumed in advance.

## Evidence

Source-described evidence mechanism:

- Assessment data produced through Events and Tools
  associated with selected Blocks.

To be established for a concrete assessment:

- The observations available to the evaluator.
- How those observations relate to the intended claim.
- Whether relevant differences could remain unobserved.
- What evidence is required beyond the reported measurements.

## Evaluator Conditions

The investigation must specify:

- Evaluator role.
- Information and instrumentation access.
- Required cooperation.
- Applicable resource constraints.
- Assumptions about evidence provenance and integrity.

These conditions have not yet been established for
a particular TEVV-Athlon assessment.

## Assumptions

No universal claim-resolution guarantee is attributed
to TEVV-Athlon.

Any model constructed for this record must state its
own admissible-world and observation assumptions.

## Structural Assurability Mapping

Relevant formal concepts include:

- Assurance context.
- Evaluator conditions and resource bounds.
- Claim-material evidence.
- Observation models.
- Observational indistinguishability.
- Claim-resolution bounds.

The theory establishes that a specified observation
model cannot resolve a claim when two admissible worlds
disagree on the claim but produce identical observations.

Applying that result to a TEVV-Athlon assessment requires
independent justification of the modeled worlds,
observations, and claims.

## Research Question

For a specified TEVV-Athlon assessment, what additional
information, if any, is needed to determine whether
its measurements can distinguish the alternatives
material to its evaluative claim?
