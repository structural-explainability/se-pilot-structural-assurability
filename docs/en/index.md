# Structural Assurability Pilot

This research pilot investigates whether claim-relative Structural
Assurability can contribute to the examination of concrete assurance
claims and external evaluation frameworks.

## Research Question

Can Structural Assurability identify useful distinctions among the
observations available to an evaluator, the evidentiary capabilities
those observations support, and the assumptions required to resolve
a specified assurance claim?

## Research Approach

Investigations begin with externally documented assurance questions.

For each question, the study identifies the relevant claim, source
material, available evidence, evaluator conditions, and assumptions.
Where applicable, these are compared with formal definitions,
theorems, and modeling limitations in Structural Assurability.

The analysis distinguishes:

- What an external source actually states.
- How that statement is interpreted for the investigation.
- What the formal theory establishes.
- What any accompanying experiment demonstrates.
- What remains uncertain or requires independent justification.

Executable experiments may be developed when they help test a
specific proposed distinction or expose a counterexample.

A finding must be supported by the examined sources and applicable
formal or experimental evidence.

## Initial Study: NIST TEVV-Athlon

The initial study examines
[NIST's TEVV-Athlon Framework for Evaluating AI Systems](https://www.nist.gov/artificial-intelligence/ai-research/tevv-athlon-framework-evaluating-ai-systems).

Previously developed mapping material provides the starting point.
The investigation examines its source provenance and relationship
to the current Structural Assurability theory.

Particular questions concern the relationship among evaluation
claims, observations, evidentiary capabilities, evaluator conditions,
and assumptions about evidence integrity and trust.

A public comment may be developed if the analysis supports a
specific issue and a useful proposed clarification.

The investigation remains valuable even if it produces no
public comment or proposed framework change.

See the
[TEVV-Athlon mapping records](https://github.com/structural-explainability/se-pilot-structural-assurability/tree/main/mappings/tevv-athlon).

## Research Boundaries

The pilot does not independently establish the safety, adequacy,
or trustworthiness of an evaluated system.

Formal results belong to the Lean theory.
Exploratory experiments establish results
only for their specified models and assumptions.

A structural-property difference does not automatically establish
a difference in evidentiary capabilities or claim resolution.
